BASIC TENNIS RULES — SUMMARY


1. OBJECTIVE AND FORMAT
Tennis is played as singles (one player against one player) or doubles (two against two). Players hit the ball over a net into the opponent’s court, trying to make it impossible for the opponent to return it legally. Each point begins with a serve and usually continues as a rally.

2. COURT BASICS
- A singles court is 27 feet wide; a doubles court is 36 feet wide.
- The serve must land in the diagonally opposite service box.
- A ball touching any part of a boundary line is considered in.

3. SERVING
- The server stands behind the baseline.
- Serves are hit diagonally into the opposite service box.
- The server alternates between the right and left sides after each point.
- The server gets two attempts. If both serves are faults, it is a double fault and the opponent wins the point.
- A serve that clips the net but lands in the correct service box is a let and is replayed.
- An ace is a legal serve the receiver cannot touch.

4. RALLIES AND LOSING A POINT
After the serve is legally returned, players hit the ball back and forth over the net.
A player generally loses the point if they:
- Fail to return the ball before it bounces twice.
- Hit the ball out of bounds.
- Hit the ball twice.
- Touch the net during the point.
- Hit the ball before it crosses the net to their side.

A ball may touch the net during a rally and still be legal if it lands inside the court.

5. SCORING
Tennis uses these point calls:
- 0 points = Love
- 1 point = 15
- 2 points = 30
- 3 points = 40
- A player normally needs at least four points and a two-point lead to win a game.

At 40–40, the score is called deuce. Under advantage scoring, a player must win two consecutive points from deuce: first to gain advantage, then to win the game. “Ad-in” means the server has the advantage; “ad-out” means the receiver has it. Some formats use no-ad scoring, so check the rules of the particular match.

6. GAMES, SETS, AND MATCHES
- A set is usually won by the first player to win six games with a lead of at least two games.
- At 6–6, a tiebreak is commonly played, depending on the format.
- Many matches are best of three sets. Some men’s Grand Slam singles matches use best of five sets.
- Players change ends after odd-numbered games, such as games 1, 3, and 5.

7. COMMON SHOTS
- Forehand: hit from the player’s dominant side.
- Backhand: hit across the body from the opposite side.
- Drop shot: a soft shot that lands just over the net.
- Smash: a forceful overhead shot.
- Slice: a shot with backspin that tends to stay low after bouncing.

8. QUICK REMINDERS
- Let the ball bounce no more than once before returning it.
- Lines count as in.
- Do not touch the net while a point is in progress.
- Alternate the serving side after every point.
- The server’s score is announced first.
- Respect opponents and officials and make fair line calls.
